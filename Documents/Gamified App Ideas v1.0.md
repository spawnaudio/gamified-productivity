# Gamified App Ideas v1.0

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 6 September 2026  
**Status:** Initial brainstorm; no concept selected.

## Purpose

Explore six gamified productivity app concepts for future discussion, comparison, and development. This document is a standalone reference for people and tools; it outlines ideas rather than a final product specification.

The central idea is to connect meaningful real-world progress with something a person can build, uncover, or move toward. The experience should remain calm and forgiving: missed days should never erase earned progress.

## 1. Tiny Town — Build a world through everyday progress

**Concept:** Completed tasks provide materials for a small, personal town. Different projects or areas of life develop different neighbourhoods.

**Core loop:** Choose a real-world task → complete it → earn materials → develop part of the town.

**Example:** Work tasks build the workshop, learning grows the library, and household tasks improve the player's home.

**Intended appeal:** A visible, personal record of effort that grows over months, with room for customisation and a sense of ownership.

**Design consideration:** The town should welcome someone back after a break and preserve everything they have built.

## 2. Boss Battle — Turn dreaded tasks into manageable encounters

**Concept:** A task someone has been avoiding becomes a boss encounter. The task is broken into a few small, concrete moves, and each completed move reduces the boss's health.

**Core loop:** Pick an avoided task → define small steps → complete each step → defeat the boss.

**Example:** Preparing an invoice or cleaning the kitchen becomes a short sequence of manageable actions.

**Intended appeal:** Make starting an unpleasant task feel more approachable and show progress before the entire task is finished.

**Optional feature:** An AI helper could suggest steps that the person can edit and confirm before using.

## 3. Focus Expedition — Travel somewhere while you work

**Concept:** Focus sessions advance a journey through a world of destinations, discoveries, and collectible souvenirs.

**Core loop:** Choose a destination and a small work objective → complete a focus session → record what moved forward → advance along the route.

**Example:** A session spent drafting a document moves the traveller toward the next location, where a discovery or souvenir awaits.

**Intended appeal:** Give repetitive work sessions a sense of adventure and continuity.

**Design consideration:** Connect advancement to a brief reflection on real progress, rather than relying only on elapsed timer time.

## 4. Desk Detective — Unlock a mystery through completed work

**Concept:** Completing meaningful tasks reveals clues in an ongoing mystery. Short story moments sit between work sessions.

**Core loop:** Choose three meaningful tasks for the day → complete a task → reveal a clue → gradually solve the case.

**Example:** Each of the day's three tasks unlocks another piece of evidence in the current investigation.

**Intended appeal:** Use curiosity and narrative continuity to encourage a return to planned work.

**Design consideration:** Cases remain available whenever the person returns. Story moments should stay brief enough to support the work rhythm.

## 5. Crew Quests — Shared missions with friends

**Concept:** A small group contributes individual productivity progress to a shared fictional mission, such as running a spaceship, restoring an island, or managing a fantasy inn.

**Core loop:** Join a shared mission → choose a personal real-world task → complete it → contribute progress → celebrate together.

**Example:** Each friend works on their own chosen task, and their completed contributions move the group's spaceship mission forward.

**Intended appeal:** Light accountability, shared celebration, and a sense of working alongside others.

**Design consideration:** Missing a session must never damage another person's progress or penalise the group.

## 6. Daily Deck — Turn planning into a small strategy game

**Concept:** Tasks become cards showing estimated effort and time. The person chooses a manageable hand for the day and can swap cards when circumstances change.

**Core loop:** Capture tasks as cards → choose a realistic daily hand → work through the cards → adjust the hand as needed.

**Example:** A daily hand includes a quick win, a main quest, and a recovery card.

**Intended appeal:** Make planning, available capacity, and trade-offs tangible and playful.

**Design consideration:** Keep the hand manageable, make adjustments easy, and treat recovery as a legitimate part of the day.

## Shared design principles

- **Reward meaningful progress.** Tie rewards to useful work and concrete steps forward.
- **Preserve earned progress.** Missed days should not erase achievements or damage the game world.
- **Keep celebrations brief.** Rewards should support the rhythm of work.
- **Welcome breaks.** Rest and returning after time away should feel comfortable.
- **Keep the person in control.** Plans can change, and any AI suggestions should be editable and confirmed before use.

## Suggested concepts to explore first

These are initial priorities for exploration, based on the proposed experience and scope. They are not a final selection or a validated market ranking.
